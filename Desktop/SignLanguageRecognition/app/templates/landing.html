AI-Based Malawi Sign Language Recognition Using Computer Vision and Machine Learning

This system recognises a verified set of static Malawi Sign Language (MSL) gestures from a standard webcam. MediaPipe extracts 21 hand landmarks per hand, the landmarks are normalised into a fixed feature vector, and a trained Random Forest classifier returns the sign together with a confidence score.

How the recognition works

The system separates data collection from real-time inference so that the exact same feature representation is used in both stages.

1. Labelled dataset collection

Administrators record samples from anonymous participants. For each captured frame only the extracted hand landmarks and their metadata are stored — never the raw video. Consent is recorded per participant before collection is allowed.

2. Participant-independent training

Frames from the same participant are never split across training and testing data. Roughly 70% of participants train the model, 15% tune it and the final 15% are held out as an independent test set.

3. Calibrated inference

A validation-derived confidence threshold rejects uncertain frames. Predictions below the threshold are reported as “Gesture not recognised” instead of being forced into a class.

Scope and limitations

  • The system recognises individual static gestures only. It does not recognise sentences, dynamic sign sequences or facial expressions.
  • Only signs suitable for static hand-pose recognition are included in the vocabulary. Signs that depend on movement or body posture are out of scope.
  • The supported vocabulary is deliberately small and grows only after verification by competent Malawi Sign Language signers.
  • Accuracy depends on the amount and diversity of the collected dataset. Published metrics are always computed from a held-out participant test set.
  • Recognition is a research prototype and must not be relied upon for safety-critical communication.

Sign descriptions in this system are placeholders for the vocabulary management workflow. They must be reviewed and confirmed against the Malawian Sign Language Dictionary and with qualified signers before the corresponding sign is marked as validated.
